# Lingo — Design Direction

## Three initial directions

### Theme Name: Midnight Atlas

Very dark editorial interface with electric language fragments, luminous navigation cues, and a calm sense of exploration.
**Probability:** 0.07

### Theme Name: Sunlit Notebook

Warm paper tones, ink-like typography, and tactile study marks that make conversation feel intimate and human.
**Probability:** 0.03

### Theme Name: Signal & Syntax

A crisp, high-contrast studio interface that treats every exchange like a live signal: precise, energetic, and quietly technical.
**Probability:** 0.08

## Chosen direction: Midnight Atlas

### Design Movement

Contemporary editorial software with a restrained neo-brutalist edge: dense dark fields, sharp typographic contrast, and intentional luminous accents used as navigational signals rather than decoration.

### Core Principles

1. Make conversation feel like the product, not a dashboard feature.
2. Use contrast and typographic scale to create momentum and confidence.
3. Treat translation, correction, and vocabulary as calm contextual layers.
4. Prefer asymmetric compositions and generous negative space over uniform card grids.

### Color Philosophy

The base is near-black ink and deep blue-black, giving the user a private, focused place to speak. A signature acid-lime accent marks moments of agency and progress, while soft cyan and violet are reserved for language signals and AI presence. Accent colors should feel like coordinates on a night map, not a gradient wallpaper.

### Layout Paradigm

A split-screen conversation cockpit: a narrow navigation rail anchors the left edge, the main conversation breathes in the center, and a contextual learning rail appears on wide screens. Marketing surfaces use editorial offsets, orbital language fragments, and oversized type rather than centered SaaS stacks.

### Signature Elements

- An acid-lime "signal dot" paired with the Lingo mark.
- Fine coordinate lines and tiny uppercase metadata labels.
- Floating language fragments that drift subtly like navigational annotations.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like opening a door into a conversation. Buttons respond with a compact press, active states are clear and calm, and learning controls reveal help without taking the user away from the exchange. Every secondary action should preserve the user's speaking rhythm.

### Animation

Use quick opacity/transform transitions under 260ms for controls. Apply a slow, barely perceptible drift to atmospheric language fragments. Stagger the first conversation messages by 60ms. Respect reduced-motion preferences and never animate layout dimensions for routine interactions.

### Typography System

Display: Space Grotesk, 600–700, for headlines and navigation anchors. Body: DM Sans, 400–600, for conversation and supporting copy. Use compact uppercase metadata in DM Sans 700 with generous tracking. Target-language text gets slightly larger sizing and generous line-height; translations are smaller and lower-contrast but remain readable.

### Brand Essence

Lingo is a conversation-first AI language studio for people who want practical fluency through real exchanges, not worksheets. Personality: **curious, lucid, encouraging**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines are direct, specific, and slightly editorial. CTAs sound like invitations to act, never like software setup. Microcopy reassures without over-explaining.

Example lines:

- “Speak first. Understand more every time.”
- “Pick a language. We’ll find the first words.”

### Wordmark & Logo

A custom lowercase wordmark built from a geometric “l” and “o” rhythm, paired with a compact orbital speech-mark: two offset lime/cyan arcs around a dark center. The symbol works alone as the favicon and conversation presence marker.

### Signature Brand Color

**Signal Lime — #D7F35B.** It is bright enough to feel ownable against ink-dark surfaces and warm enough to communicate encouragement rather than warning.

## Style Decisions

- Signal Lime #D7F35B is reserved for agency, progress, and key navigational signals; it stays concentrated against ink-dark surfaces.
- The Lingo wordmark uses a custom geometric lowercase rhythm with a recognizable orbital speech-mark detail rather than a default typed brand name.
- Every major section carries at least one Midnight Atlas cue: coordinate lines, compact uppercase metadata, numbered wayfinding, orbital language fragments, or signal-style contextual annotations.
- The conversation cockpit remains the visual center of gravity, with translation, vocabulary, progress, and tutor notes treated as distinct contextual layers.
